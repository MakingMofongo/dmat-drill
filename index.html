How to work each part

Figure sequences

  • Track one figure at a time and knock out options with it. One figure usually removes one or two of the three choices, so you rarely need them all.
  • Check in this order: where it moves (row or column bounce, diagonal bounce, clockwise or anticlockwise round the border, 1 or 2 steps), how it turns (90° right or left), then colour (alternating 2 or cycling 3).
  • x + 1 rule: steps grow 1, 2, 3, 4… From matrix 4 to 5 it moves 4 steps, and from 5 to 6 it moves 5. Rotation can grow the same way.
  • At an edge a figure either bounces back or runs along the border. Figures never overlap and never leave the grid.

Equations

  • Every letter is a whole number from 1 to 20. Use that bound: 5 × B = A means B is at most 4.
  • Find the equation that pins a letter directly. If none does, substitute every definition into the equation that uses all the letters (the long one), solve for the single letter left, then back-substitute.
  • Keep signs straight when you substitute into a minus: A − (11 + B) = A − 11 − B.
  • Check one equation before confirming. It costs 5 seconds.

Latin squares

  • First, list the letters in the ? cell's row and column together. If four different letters appear, the fifth is the answer, often in 10 seconds.
  • If not, look for a cell whose row and column together already hold four letters and fill it. Also look for a letter that fits only one cell of a row or column.
  • Fill only what gets you nearer the ?. You never need the whole grid.

Subject module (90 min)

  • At the start, count the texts and split the 90 minutes. With 4 texts that is about 20 minutes each, plus a buffer.
  • Skim the text for its structure, then answer from the text. The answer is always derivable from the text plus school maths and logic, not from outside facts.
  • Typical moves: scale a formula (a square root halves or doubles by √2), read a definition exactly, pick the one statement true in all cases, and spot a design that breaks the stated method.
  • Out of your field (physics, economics, social science) is normal. Everyone is out of field on most texts.

Across the whole test

  • 75 seconds per core item. If an item passes two minutes, guess and move on. A hard item scores the same as an easy one.
  • Answer every item before time runs out. A blank is a sure zero, and a guess gets 1 in 3 on figures and 1 in 4 on texts.
